Who Build AI Agents and How Do They Interact?

By Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-16 · 2 min read

AI agents are created by developers, research institutions, and tech companies specializing in artificial intelligence. These builders design agents to perform specific tasks or collaborate, sometimes making them interact with each other to enhance capabilities. Understanding who builds these agents and how they communicate helps in developing smarter systems.

Who Builds AI Agents

AI agents are primarily built by software developers, AI researchers, and technology companies. Major tech firms such as Google, Microsoft, and Facebook develop AI agents for applications ranging from virtual assistants to complex data analysis. Universities also contribute through research projects focused on multi-agent systems and autonomous agents.

Startups and independent developers create specialized or open-source AI agents focused on niche applications. These builders often share their work on platforms like GitHub, enabling collaborative improvements and innovations.

  • Technology companies often have dedicated AI research teams.
  • Universities publish papers that influence AI agent development.
  • Open-source communities provide tools for building and experimenting with AI agents.

How AI Agents Are Made to Interact

AI agents communicate through predefined protocols, message-passing interfaces, or shared data environments. Developers can make two or more AI agents talk to each other by designing interaction rules and communication channels.

For example, multi-agent systems often use messaging protocols like FIPA ACL or custom APIs to facilitate cooperation and coordination. This enables agents to share information, negotiate, and work on tasks collectively.

Making Two AI Agents Talk to Each Other

To enable two AI agents to communicate, developers define a communication protocol. This might involve setting up message formats, topics, and responses. The agents then send messages through APIs or messaging queues like RabbitMQ or Kafka.

For instance, in a supply chain scenario, an inventory management agent might request restocking information from a supplier agent. They exchange structured messages to coordinate actions without human intervention.

Edge Cases and Exceptions in AI Communication

While making AI agents talk to each other is powerful, it can lead to unexpected behaviors. Agents might misunderstand messages, conflict, or create unintended feedback loops.

For example, in multi-agent systems, conflicting goals or misaligned communication protocols can cause agents to act against the intended outcome. Handling these edge cases requires careful design and monitoring.

  • Miscommunication can cause system failures.
  • Agents might develop unforeseen strategies.
  • Security concerns arise if malicious agents interfere.

Managing Unintended Interactions

Developers implement safeguards such as validation, error handling, and fallback protocols. Regular audits and simulations help identify potential issues before deployment.

Frequently asked questions about who build ai agents

Can AI agents interact with each other automatically?

Yes, AI agents can interact automatically through predefined communication protocols, enabling them to coordinate, negotiate, and perform tasks together without human input.

How do you make two AI agents talk to each other?

You set up communication protocols using messaging formats and APIs, then program the agents to send and receive messages based on specific rules and shared environments.

Are there limits to how many AI agents can communicate?

While technically possible to scale communication to many agents, practical constraints include system resources, protocol complexity, and managing unintended interactions or conflicts.

What are common methods for AI agents to collaborate?

Common methods include message-passing protocols, shared data repositories, and coordinating through multi-agent platforms that facilitate real-time interaction and task division.

Who is responsible for building AI agents used in industry?

Responsible entities include large tech companies, research institutions, and specialized startups, all developing AI agents tailored for automation, customer service, or data analysis.

Understanding who build AI agents and how they communicate is crucial for deploying effective intelligent systems.
