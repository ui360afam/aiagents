When was agentic AI introduced? A complete guide

By Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-16 · 2 min read

The question of when agentic AI was introduced is fundamental to understanding its evolution. Agentic AI refers to systems capable of autonomous decision-making and interaction. This article explores the history, key developments, and how AI agents now communicate. We also discuss the exceptions and edge cases in AI agent interactions.

Key takeaways

  • Agentic AI emerged in the 1960s with early autonomous concepts
  • Multi-agent systems gained prominence in the 1980s
  • AI agents can communicate via message protocols and shared data
  • Interactions enhance system efficiency but face security and technical limits
  • Understanding the history helps anticipate future AI developments

Historical Timeline of Agentic AI Development

The concept of autonomous agents dates back to the 1960s. Early AI researchers envisioned systems capable of independent action. The 1980s saw the emergence of multi-agent systems that could communicate and cooperate. Around the 2000s, frameworks for agent-based AI became more sophisticated, leading to modern autonomous AI systems.

  • 1960s: Basic autonomous agent ideas emerge
  • 1980s: Development of multi-agent systems
  • 2000s: Formalization of agent architectures
  • 2010s: Commercial and practical deployment of autonomous AI

Early Autonomous Systems

Initial ideas focused on simple robots and software agents that could perform tasks independently. Researchers explored basic decision-making processes, laying the groundwork for agentic AI.

Multi-Agent System Evolution

In the 1980s, multi-agent systems allowed multiple agents to interact, coordinate, and negotiate. These systems demonstrated how autonomous agents can work together to solve complex problems.

Can AI Agents Interact With Each Other?

Yes, AI agents can interact with each other. This is a core feature of multi-agent systems where agents communicate, negotiate, and coordinate actions. Techniques like message passing, shared environment states, and predefined protocols enable these interactions.

  • Agents communicate via message protocols
  • Interactions can be cooperative or competitive
  • Communication methods include direct messaging and shared data
  • Agents can adapt based on interactions

How to Make Two AI Agents Talk to Each Other

To enable two AI agents to talk, developers establish communication protocols. These can be message-passing interfaces, shared memory, or API calls. Setting clear protocols ensures meaningful exchanges and coordination.

Examples of AI Agent Communication

In autonomous vehicle fleets, cars communicate to coordinate routes. In financial trading, agents negotiate deals based on market data. These interactions improve efficiency and decision-making.

Edge Cases and Exceptions in AI Agent Interactions

While AI agents generally communicate effectively, there are exceptions. Some systems lack communication capabilities, or interactions are limited due to security concerns or technical constraints. Edge cases include non-communicative agents or incompatible protocols.

  • Some AI agents operate independently without interaction
  • Security restrictions may limit communication between agents
  • Incompatibility in communication protocols can prevent interaction
  • Complex environments may hinder agent cooperation

Non-Communicative Agents

Certain AI systems are designed for solitary tasks, such as single-use automation tools, and do not require interaction. These are exceptions to typical multi-agent frameworks.

Technical and Security Limitations

Protocols must be secure to prevent malicious interference. In some cases, technical limitations or proprietary formats restrict communication between different AI systems.

The bottom line

The development of agentic AI has evolved over decades, from simple autonomous systems to complex multi-agent interactions. To explore current applications or develop your own AI agents, consider studying communication protocols and interaction strategies. Next steps include experimenting with multi-agent frameworks and staying updated on technological advances.
