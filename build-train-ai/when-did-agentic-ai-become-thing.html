When Did Agentic AI Become a Thing?

By Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-16 · 3 min read

The development of agentic AI has been a gradual process, beginning with early automation systems and evolving into autonomous agents capable of decision-making. This article explores when agentic AI first appeared and how it has grown over time. Understanding this history helps in grasping current capabilities and future prospects.

Key takeaways

  • Agentic AI evolved from early expert systems in the 1970s.
  • The term 'agent' became popular in AI literature during the 1990s.
  • Modern agentic AI relies heavily on deep learning and reinforcement learning.
  • There are systems called 'agents' that are not truly autonomous or learning-based.

The Origins of Agentic AI

Agentic AI's roots trace back to the 1970s and 1980s with the development of expert systems and autonomous robots. These early systems could perform specific tasks without human intervention, representing the first steps toward autonomous decision-making.

In the 1990s, the concept of intelligent agents gained traction within the computer science community. Researchers focused on multi-agent systems designed to work together to solve complex problems, laying the groundwork for more autonomous AI.

  • 1970s: Expert systems and autonomous robots emerge
  • 1980s: Development of intelligent software agents
  • 1990s: Multi-agent systems become a research focus

Early Examples and Milestones

One of the earliest examples was the Mycin system in the 1970s, which could diagnose bacterial infections. Although limited, it demonstrated decision-making capabilities without human input.

In robotics, systems like Stanford's Shakey robot in the late 1960s and early 1970s could perform basic navigation tasks independently.

When Did the Term 'Agent' Enter AI Literature?

The specific term 'agent' gained popularity in AI literature during the 1990s, particularly with the growth of multi-agent systems and distributed AI. Researchers aimed to model autonomous entities that could perceive, decide, and act within environments.

This period marked a shift from task-specific automation to more flexible, adaptable agents capable of handling complex, dynamic situations.

  • 1990s: 'Agent' term becomes mainstream in AI research
  • Focus on autonomous, interactive entities
  • Development of frameworks like the Java Agent Development Framework (JADE)

The Rise of Multi-Agent Systems

Multi-agent systems enabled multiple agents to collaborate or compete, simulating complex social interactions. These systems found applications in simulation, logistics, and network management.

The concept of an agent as an autonomous, goal-directed entity became central to AI research and practical applications.

The Explosion of Agentic AI in the 2010s

The 2010s saw what many consider the true emergence of agentic AI, driven by advances in deep learning, natural language processing, and cloud computing. AI agents like virtual assistants and autonomous vehicles showcased high levels of independence and adaptability.

This era marked a shift from static rule-based systems to learning-based agents capable of improving their performance over time.

  • 2010s: Introduction of intelligent virtual assistants like Siri and Alexa
  • Development of autonomous vehicles with decision-making capabilities
  • Integration of reinforcement learning allowing agents to adapt
YearMajor Milestones

Key Technologies Enabling Modern Agentic AI

Deep neural networks allowed AI to interpret complex data and make decisions with minimal human input.

Reinforcement learning enabled agents to learn optimal actions through trial and error, leading to more autonomous behavior.

Exceptions and Edge Cases in the History of Agentic AI

While the milestones above mark significant points, some systems predate or fall outside typical definitions of agentic AI. For instance, simple automation scripts or rule-based systems perform tasks without true autonomy or decision-making.

Certain AI systems labeled as 'agents' do not possess learning or perception capabilities and are limited to predefined functions.

  • Rule-based chatbots with scripted responses
  • Automated trading bots following fixed algorithms
  • Legacy systems with hard-coded decision trees

What Differentiates True Agentic AI?

True agentic AI can perceive environments, learn from interactions, and adapt behaviors accordingly. Systems without these features are not considered genuinely agentic, despite sometimes being called 'agents.'

Frequently asked questions about when did agentic ai become a thing

When did the first autonomous AI agents appear?

Early autonomous AI systems appeared in the 1970s with expert systems like Mycin and robots such as Shakey, which could perform tasks without human control.

How do I start building an AI agent?

Begin by defining the agent's purpose, then choose suitable algorithms, gather data, and train your model. Use frameworks like OpenAI Gym or TensorFlow to develop and test your AI agent.

What is the difference between an AI agent and a regular AI system?

An AI agent perceives its environment, makes decisions, and acts independently based on goals. Regular AI systems follow predefined rules without perception or autonomous decision-making.

Can I create an agentic AI without deep learning?

Yes, traditional rule-based systems can be agentic if they perceive and act based on predefined rules, but deep learning improves adaptability and learning capabilities.

When did agentic AI become mainstream?

Agentic AI gained mainstream attention in the 2010s with advances in machine learning, virtual assistants, and autonomous vehicles demonstrating high levels of independence.

The bottom line

Understanding the history of agentic AI helps clarify its current capabilities and future potential. If you want to develop your own AI agent, start by learning about machine learning frameworks and defining clear goals. Exploring existing systems can provide valuable insights into creating autonomous, adaptive AI.
