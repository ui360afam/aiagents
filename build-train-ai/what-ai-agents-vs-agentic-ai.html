What is AI Agents vs Agentic AI: Clear Explanation

By Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-16 · 3 min read

AI agents are software entities that perceive their environment and act to achieve specific goals. Agentic AI refers to systems that exhibit autonomous decision-making with human-like agency. Understanding these distinctions helps in developing effective AI solutions.

Key takeaways

  • AI agents follow predefined rules or learned behaviors to perform tasks.
  • Agentic AI can make independent decisions and adapt to new situations.
  • Building an AI agent involves data collection, algorithm selection, and testing.
  • Training methods vary from supervised learning to reinforcement learning.
  • Safety and oversight are crucial for highly autonomous systems.

Understanding AI Agents and Agentic AI

AI agents are programs designed to perform specific tasks by perceiving their environment and taking actions based on predefined rules or learned behaviors. They operate within boundaries set by their developers.

Agentic AI pushes beyond simple automation. It involves systems that can make autonomous decisions, learn from interactions, and adapt without direct human intervention. The key difference lies in the level of autonomy and decision-making capability.

  • AI agents follow programmed rules or learned policies.
  • Agentic AI can generate novel responses and adapt dynamically.
  • Both types are used in automation, robotics, and data analysis.

How to Build an AI Agent

Building an AI agent involves defining its purpose, gathering data, choosing the right algorithms, and deploying the system. For example, creating a chatbot requires training it on conversation datasets.

Start with understanding the environment and the actions the agent needs to perform. Use tools like Python, TensorFlow, or PyTorch to implement machine learning models.

How to Train an AI Agent

Training involves feeding data into algorithms so the agent can learn to perform tasks. Supervised learning uses labeled datasets, while reinforcement learning involves rewarding desirable actions.

For autonomous vehicles, reinforcement learning helps the system improve decision-making based on feedback from the environment.

Differences and Edge Cases

While AI agents operate based on programmed rules or learned models, agentic AI introduces a degree of autonomy that enables decision-making beyond explicit instructions. For instance, a simple AI agent might respond to a user query, but an agentic AI could proactively suggest solutions based on context.

Exceptions include hybrid systems where agentic features are integrated into traditional agents. Some AI systems may temporarily act autonomously but revert to human oversight under certain conditions.

  • Agentic AI can exhibit behaviors that are unpredictable and highly autonomous.
  • In edge cases, AI systems may switch between agentic and non-agentic modes depending on safety or context.
  • Designing for safety involves implementing fail-safes and transparency in decision processes.

Edge Cases in AI Autonomy

Autonomous weapons systems, for example, are complex edge cases where safety and ethical considerations are critical. These systems might operate with high autonomy but require strict oversight.

In customer support, hybrid systems combine scripted responses with AI-generated suggestions, balancing automation and human control.

How to Start Creating and Training Your AI Agent

Begin by clearly defining the problem your AI agent will solve. Collect relevant data and choose appropriate algorithms. Use development frameworks like TensorFlow or PyTorch to prototype and test your system.

To train your AI agent effectively, start with small datasets and gradually increase complexity. Validate its performance regularly and fine-tune parameters to improve accuracy.

  • Define the task and gather quality data.
  • Select suitable machine learning models or reinforcement learning algorithms.
  • Test in controlled environments before deployment.
  • Continuously monitor and update your AI agent post-deployment.

Frequently asked questions about what is ai agents vs agentic ai

What is the main difference between an AI agent and agentic AI?

An AI agent performs tasks based on predefined rules or learned models, while agentic AI can make autonomous decisions and act independently in complex situations.

How can I create an AI agent for my business?

Identify the problem, gather relevant data, select suitable algorithms, and use frameworks like TensorFlow to build and train your agent. Start small and expand as you validate performance.

What are common methods to train an AI agent?

Supervised learning, reinforcement learning, and unsupervised learning are typical methods, each suited to different tasks like classification, autonomous control, or pattern detection.

Can I make an AI agent autonomous without it becoming agentic AI?

Yes, by designing strict boundaries and safety measures, you can limit autonomy. Most systems remain simple agents, but adding complex decision-making capabilities can lead to agentic behavior.

What are the safety concerns with agentic AI?

Autonomous decision-making can cause unpredictable outcomes. Implementing safety protocols, transparency, and oversight mechanisms are essential to mitigate risks.

The bottom line

Understanding the distinction between AI agents and agentic AI helps in selecting the right approach for your project. To advance further, explore tutorials on creating and training AI agents, and always prioritize safety when developing autonomous systems.
