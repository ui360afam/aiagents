What Are AI Agents and Agentic AI?

AI agents are software programs that perform tasks on behalf of users, often using artificial intelligence to make decisions. Agentic AI refers to systems that exhibit autonomy, goal-directed behavior, and adaptability, making decisions without human intervention.

Typically, AI agents operate within predefined rules or learning models, while agentic AI can set its own goals based on environmental inputs.

  • AI agents can be simple or complex, from chatbots to autonomous vehicles.
  • Agentic AI emphasizes autonomy and goal orientation.
  • Both operate using machine learning, natural language processing, and other AI techniques.

Differences Between AI Agents and Agentic AI

Standard AI agents follow specific instructions and cannot deviate significantly from their programming. In contrast, agentic AI demonstrates a higher level of independence, often setting its own priorities and strategies.

For example, a chatbot responding to user queries is an AI agent, but an autonomous drone navigating unpredictably in a city is an agentic AI.

Are AI Agents and Bots the Same?

Many confuse AI agents with bots, but they are not identical. Bots typically are automated scripts that perform repetitive tasks, often with limited decision-making ability. AI agents can include bots but are generally more sophisticated.

For example, a simple web crawler is a bot, while an AI-powered customer service agent that understands context and learns over time qualifies as an AI agent.

  • Bots are often rule-based and reactive.
  • AI agents can adapt and learn from data.
  • Not all bots are AI-based; some are purely scripted.

Common Types of AI Bots and Agents

Rule-based chatbots handle simple FAQs. AI chatbots incorporate natural language processing and machine learning.

Autonomous AI agents can operate independently, such as trading bots that adapt to market changes or virtual assistants that manage schedules.

Are AI Agents and Chatbots the Same?

Most chatbots are a subset of AI agents but are not always classified as agentic AI. Basic chatbots follow scripts and respond to predefined inputs, lacking true autonomy.

Advanced AI chatbots, like those used by large companies, can understand context, learn from interactions, and perform more complex tasks, blurring the lines between chatbots and AI agents.

  • Simple chatbots are rule-based, not autonomous.
  • Advanced chatbots use AI to simulate human-like conversations.
  • Both can be AI agents if they perform autonomous tasks.

Examples of AI Chatbots versus Basic Chatbots

A basic website FAQ chatbot answers standard questions. An AI chatbot on a customer support platform can troubleshoot issues and escalate problems intelligently.
